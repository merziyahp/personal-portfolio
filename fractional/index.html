How I Work · The Missing Reality

Every struggling roadmap is missing a specific reality.

A user whose actual workflow nobody mapped. A buyer who was never asked to pay. A risk everyone agreed not to look at. My practice is built on finding it - through two lenses.

Lens 01

Whose reality is missing?

I map the requirements against the people they were written for - then against the people who actually use and pay. The gap between those two is usually where the traction went.

Lens 02

What's riskiest to leave unfixed?

Every plan has one assumption that, if wrong, sinks the rest. I find the point where the build diverges most from confirmed reality and derisk that first. Not the easiest fix. The riskiest gap.

Some of my strongest catches happened before a line of code was written. For funded, post-build companies, I apply the same lens retroactively: where did what you built diverge from what's real, and what happens if you leave it?

And the fix is usually smaller than you fear. I work iteratively - small, precise corrections that bring the system you have up to level, not a rip-and-replace of your roadmap or your process. Overhauls are how consultants justify themselves. Small tweaks that hold are how products actually get healthy.

Case Studies

Realities I've found.

Hematogenix

Clinical lab platform
The Missing Reality

The requirements arrived handed-down and looking complete. I verified them against the people they'd actually land on and found the data entry technicians had never been part of the conversation - the new design would have made their work slower and more error-prone, and nobody would have known until after launch.

What Changed

Incorporated their reality in the smallest change possible, negotiated the scope with the client and execs, and delivered. No major rework. And the technicians were happy - which is rarer than it sounds.

GameChanger

Youth sports · millions of users
The Missing Reality

The obvious plan was to rebuild the mobile app on the web. Research said users didn't need a rebuild - they needed specific workflows first, which gave us a roadmap for fast delivery instead of a long port.

What Changed

First web version shipped in 2 months, expanded as the season progressed. Usage doubled in year one; support tickets dropped 30%.

VaultBeauty

Two-sided beauty marketplace
The Missing Reality

Research pointed to an uncomfortable possibility - the segment the product was aimed at wasn't the segment that would pay. Instead of betting the roadmap on a hunch, we built a simple experiment into the MVP to confirm it.

What Changed

Pivot confirmed, moved upmarket, 2x revenue per user.

TwentyEight Health

The Missing Reality

What Changed
